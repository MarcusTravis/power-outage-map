The source told 964media that work was scheduled to begin Sunday on the KM250 section, with a production capacity of 250 million cubic feet of gas a day; that the attacked KM400 section, which had a daily production capacity of 500 million cubic feet, had not yet had any maintenance work; and that repairing the attacked section, if work on it began, would take at least four to five months before it could start production. The same source said that of the 250 million cubic feet from the KM250 section, 150 million cubic feet had previously gone to consumption inside the Kurdistan Region and 100 million to electricity in Iraq, that it was not yet clear whether, once work resumed, the Iraqi government would allow the entire 250 million cubic feet to go to the Kurdistan Region, and that if the entire 250 million cubic feet were allocated to the Kurdistan Region, electricity supply there could rise to about 12 hours a day. The report said Khor Mor had produced 750 million cubic feet of gas a day before the attack, so 250 million cubic feet would equal one-third of its previous capacity, and that after the Nov 8 drone attack electricity supply in the Kurdistan Region had fallen from 24 hours a day to between 6 and 8 hours. Those statements were from an unnamed technical source and were not an official confirmation from Dana Gas or from the Kurdistan Region's electricity ministry that production had already resumed or that a Sunday restart was guaranteed. The New Region, in a Nov 23, 2026 report, had said gas production at the Khor Mor field was expected to resume in the coming days after a Monday meeting of delegations from Erbil and Baghdad with the field's operator, Dana Gas; that, according to information acquired by The New Region, the outcome of the meeting was positive, with Dana Gas set to resume gas production in the next two to three days; and that a source at the field told The New Region that all employees at Khor Mor had returned to work, that Dana Gas was set to release a statement on the resumption of operations in the near future, that efforts to repair the damaged sections were also expected to begin soon, and that production was expected to resume within the next 48 hours in areas unaffected by the attack. Omed Ahmed, spokesperson for the Kurdistan Region's electricity ministry, told The New Region on Monday that electricity generation at power stations was expected to resume within hours of gas production restarting, with supply gradually increasing, but that it could take up to 24 hours of normal operations for electricity production to return to pre-attack levels. Those earlier reports did not say that Khor Mor production or pre-attack electricity supply had already resumed. Kurdistan24, in a Nov 22, 2026 report, said the Nov 8 drone attack in Sulaimani province caused a large fire and stopped gas production entirely, with no casualties, and that the stoppage cut 2,500 to 2,800 megawatts of electricity in the Kurdistan Region and reduced power-supply hours by five to six hours a day. That account is consistent with earlier official figures: Al Jazeera, on Nov 9, citing Iraqi and Kurdish officials and the official Iraqi News Agency, said the attack left more than 70 percent of the Kurdish region without power and, citing Omed Ahmed, that it suspended gas supply to power plants in Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Bazian, Chamchamal, and Duhok and knocked between 2,500 and 2,800 megawatts off the grid. Rudaw later quoted Ahmed as saying authorities had increased generation from approximately 1,000 megawatts immediately after the attack to 1,550 megawatts, of which 50 megawatts were reserved for essential facilities and factories, leaving 1,500 megawatts for residents and raising daily public electricity supply to six to eight hours; Ahmed said the electricity then available covered only 40 percent of demand, leaving a 60 percent shortfall, and that no date had been established for production to resume. Rudaw's article image assets for that report are dated Nov 13, 2026. 964media, in a Nov 15 report, quoted Ahmed as saying that no timeframe had been set for supplies to return to normal and that within 48 hours of the attack generation had been increased from 1,000 megawatts to nearly 1,650 megawatts. Dana Gas, in a Nov 14 clarification later restated in coverage including a Kurdistan24 report, said the facility had sustained significant damage and that no gas could currently be produced. The Insight International, in a Nov 21 report citing private-generator officials who spoke to Rudaw on Saturday, Nov 21, said the public grid remained weak because of the gas shortfall and that neighborhood generators were still running for most of the day in Erbil, Sulaimani, and Duhok.
