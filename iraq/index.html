Omed Ahmed, spokesperson for the Kurdistan Region's electricity ministry, told The New Region on Monday that electricity generation at power stations was expected to resume within hours of gas production restarting, with supply gradually increasing, but that it could take up to 24 hours of normal operations for electricity production to return to pre-attack levels. Those reports did not say that Khor Mor production or pre-attack electricity supply had already resumed. An earlier Nov 22, 2026 report by The New Region had said the Monday meeting was planned to discuss resuming production, that Dana Gas was expected to insist on security guarantees against further attacks, and that if an agreement were reached on Monday, a specialized Turkish company would begin repairs estimated to take six days. Kurdistan24, in a Nov 22, 2026 report, said the Nov 8 drone attack in Sulaimani province caused a large fire and stopped gas production entirely, with no casualties, and that the stoppage cut 2,500 to 2,800 megawatts of electricity in the Kurdistan Region and reduced power-supply hours by five to six hours a day. That account is consistent with earlier official figures: Al Jazeera, on Nov 9, citing Iraqi and Kurdish officials and the official Iraqi News Agency, said the attack left more than 70 percent of the Kurdish region without power and, citing Omed Ahmed, that it suspended gas supply to power plants in Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Bazian, Chamchamal, and Duhok and knocked between 2,500 and 2,800 megawatts off the grid. Rudaw later quoted Ahmed as saying authorities had increased generation from approximately 1,000 megawatts immediately after the attack to 1,550 megawatts, of which 50 megawatts were reserved for essential facilities and factories, leaving 1,500 megawatts for residents and raising daily public electricity supply to six to eight hours; Ahmed said the electricity then available covered only 40 percent of demand, leaving a 60 percent shortfall, and that no date had been established for production to resume. Rudaw's article image assets for that report are dated Nov 13, 2026. 964media, in a Nov 15 report, quoted Ahmed as saying that no timeframe had been set for supplies to return to normal and that within 48 hours of the attack generation had been increased from 1,000 megawatts to nearly 1,650 megawatts. Dana Gas, in a Nov 14 clarification later restated in coverage including a Kurdistan24 report, said the facility had sustained significant damage and that no gas could currently be produced. The Insight International, in a Nov 21 report citing private-generator officials who spoke to Rudaw on Saturday, Nov 21, said the public grid remained weak because of the gas shortfall and that neighborhood generators were still running for most of the day in Erbil, Sulaimani, and Duhok.
