Kyiv and nationwideUkraineRolling and emergency blackouts after Russian attacks on energy infrastructureUkrenergo said planned electricity outages would be in effect around the clock on Tuesday, Nov 17, in parts of Ukraine: capacity-restriction schedules for industry, and hourly outage schedules of 1 to 3 groups (queues) for all categories of consumers, because of the aftermath of attacks on the energy system. LB.ua, citing Ukrenergo, published that update at 10:18 p.m. EET Monday. UNN, citing Ukrenergo in an 8:07 p.m. EET Monday report, said one group equals four hours without electricity, so Tuesday's scheduled outages may last from 4 to 12 hours; it said up-to-date schedules should be checked on each region's distribution-system operator. On the evening of Monday, Nov 16, Ukrenergo said enemy strikes had caused large-scale power outages in eastern Ukraine and that the situation was particularly difficult in Dnipro, Kharkiv, Poltava, and Zaporizhzhia, where electricity supply was severely restricted because of significant damage to energy infrastructure. As of the morning of Tuesday, Nov 17, the citywide Monday-evening blackout was no longer in effect for the whole city, but emergency outages continued in parts of Dnipro and nearby areas. Dnepr.info, in a 7:27 a.m. EET Tuesday report, said electricity had been restored for some residents, but that scheduled stabilization outages had not yet been introduced in the hardest-hit areas. Citing DTEK Dniprovski Elektromerezhi, it said emergency outages remained in effect, by Ukrenergo order, in parts of the cities of Dnipro, Kamianske, and Samar, as well as in the Samarivskyi and Dniprovskyi districts, Pavlohrad and the Pavlohrad district, and Solone and Tsarychanka, while scheduled stabilization outages were in effect in the rest of the region. DTEK said crews were already restoring supply, returning power first to water and heating systems and social facilities, and warned that further Russian attacks could slow the work. Dnipro Mayor Borys Filatov, in a Monday Telegram post reported by NV at 7:10 p.m. EET, said that after Russian attacks on the city, almost half of Dnipro was without electricity, and he called it the sixth blackout in two weeks. He also said two people were killed, eight were in hospital, and 10 apartment buildings were damaged. Ukrenergo, as reported by Ukrinform at 7:30 p.m. EET Monday, said the situation was particularly difficult in Dnipro, Kharkiv, Poltava, and Zaporizhzhia, where electricity supply was severely restricted because of significant damage to energy infrastructure. Mariupol, Donetsk, Volnovakha, Novoazovsk, and nearby citiesRussian-occupied Donetsk region, UkraineOccupation authorities said energy infrastructure was attacked on the nights of Nov 15 and Nov 16, causing an emergency in the regional power system. On Nov 15, the Russia-appointed head of the Donetsk occupation government, Andrey Chertkov, said the grid was damaged by a "powerful enemy attack," while some Russian media called it an accident. Vedomosti reported that Ukrainian forces struck energy infrastructure in the region overnight Nov 15Occupation authorities have not given a single independently confirmed total, and the latest official figures they issued still come from occupation sources. Donetsk Agency News (DAN), in an 8:30 p.m. local Nov 16 report, citing the occupation administration of the head and government of the occupied Donetsk region and Donetskenergo director Oleg Pavlyak, said 428,000 subscribers remained without electricity. Of those, it said 284,000 were de-energized because of the emergency shutdown of a power unit, and 144,000 household consumers were under a temporary capacity-limitation schedule (GVO) of 95 MW. It said the unit was expected to start by midnight, after which specialists planned to begin restoring supply to consumers in Donetsk, Mariupol, and the Novoazovsk, Volnovakha, and Novotroitsk districts. News-donetsk.ru, in an 8:14 a.m. local Tuesday, Nov 17 report citing the occupation Ministry of Coal and Energy, said energy workers had carried out the necessary restoration work and that most previously de-energized consumers had already been reconnected. It said the regional power system remained under strain, that because of a shortage of available capacity and high grid load, forced temporary outages were possible in Donetsk, Horlivka, and Yasynuvata, and that local outages were also occurring in Makiivka, Yenakiieve, Novoazovsk, and Shakhtarsk municipal districts. It said the outages were emergency in nature, were not planned in advance, and were introduced based on the current state of the grids and the load; it did not give a new customer count. Earlier Monday morning figures, which have been superseded by the later Monday evening and Tuesday morning updates, included 201,220 subscribers without electricity in all districts of Donetsk, according to occupation-appointed mayor Alexey Kulemzin citing Donetskenergo, as reported by news-donetsk.ru at 10:55 a.m. local Nov 16, and more than 133,500 subscribers without electricity in Mariupol, with 941 transformer substations in the urban district still de-energized, according to the occupation administration of the Mariupol urban district as reported by MRPL.NEWS at 9:41 a.m. local Nov 16. These figures come from occupation authorities and were not independently confirmed in the sources reviewed.
