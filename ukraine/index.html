Kyiv and nationwideUkraineRolling and emergency blackouts after Russian attacks on energy infrastructureUkrenergo said that on Monday, Nov 16, hourly rolling blackouts of 1 to 3 queues for all consumers, plus industrial capacity limits, are in effect around the clock in parts of Ukraine, tighter than Sunday's 1 to 2 queues. UNN, citing Ukrenergo, said one queue is four hours, so Monday's outages may last from 4 to 12 hours. UA.News, also citing Ukrenergo, said the Monday schedules apply throughout the day in certain regions. No later official Ukrenergo Monday schedule change was found. On Sunday afternoon, Ukrenergo said a Russian attack damaged energy equipment and that emergency power outages had been introduced in the Zaporizhzhia and Dnipropetrovsk regions. Mykhailo Semikin, acting head of the Zaporizhzhia Regional Military Administration, said Sunday evening that power supply had been restored in the Zaporizhzhia region after the attack, according to Ukrinform at 9:00 p.m. EET. No later official customer count or restoration notice was found for the Dnipropetrovsk emergency outages. DTEK said some consumers in the cities of Dnipro and Kamianske, and in the Dnipro and Kamianske districts, were left without power, that crews were restoring supply where the security situation permitted, and that it did not give a customer count. Kamianske mayor Andriy Bilousov, in a Sunday evening update reported by 5692.com.ua, said outage schedules were not in effect, that electricity was being supplied first to critical infrastructure, that repair crews were working continuously, and that the pace of restoration also depended on security conditions; he gave no customer count and did not say the city had been fully restored. Overnight outages: DTEK Odeski Elektromerezhi said in a Nov 15 statement that a Russian attack overnight on one of its energy facilities left 41,000 customers without power (no restoration time given; DTEK said crews began work once it was safe, that they were clearing rubble and repairing damaged equipment, that restoration will take time, and that air-raid alerts were significantly complicating the work). Oleh Kiper, head of the Odesa Regional Military Administration, also said around 41,000 customers were temporarily left without electricity after a strike on a DTEK Odesa Electricity Networks facility; Ukraine's State Emergency Service said Russia attacked Chornomorsk, and reporting on the same overnight attack said the city was left without power. No later official customer count or restoration notice was found. Prior Saturday outages with no later official restoration notice found: DTEK Odeski Elektromerezhi said a Russian attack on one of its facilities in the north of Odesa region on the morning of Nov 14 left almost 50,000 customers without power (no restoration time given; DTEK said the facility had been hit repeatedly and that air-raid alerts were complicating the work). Mariupol, Donetsk, Volnovakha, Novoazovsk, and nearby citiesRussian-occupied Donetsk region, UkraineOccupation authorities described an emergency in the regional power system. The Russia-appointed head of the Donetsk occupation government, Andrey Chertkov, said the grid was damaged by a "powerful enemy attack," while some Russian media called it an accident. Vedomosti reported that Ukrainian forces struck energy infrastructure in the region overnight Nov 15No official customer count was given in the Nov 15 statement by the occupation Ministry of Coal and Energy, which said there was a partial power outage in the Donetsk, Mariupol, and Dokuchaevsk urban districts and in the Novoazovsk, Telmanove, and Volnovakha municipal districts. The ministry said crews had begun emergency restoration work and that, if conditions were favorable, a phased restoration of supply was planned by night. It also said that because of a shortage of capacity in the regional power system, supply was being limited in a number of districts, including parts of the Shakhtarsk municipal district, and that power would be restored as the system stabilized and technical conditions allowed. Vedomosti, citing the same ministry, also said that after overnight strikes on energy infrastructure, morning outages on Nov 15 had already left parts of Donetsk's Petrovskyi district, the Snizhne urban district, one settlement in the Amvrosiivka district, and part of the Yenakiieve urban district without power. Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty, citing Chertkov, said the first substations might return to service overnight, while other occupation authorities, including the ministry, said the damage was extensive and that it was not yet possible to determine when power would be restored. In a later Sunday evening statement carried by dnrnews.ru at 8:18 p.m. local, Chertkov said the damage was serious, that it was difficult to say how long restoration would take, and that by a preliminary estimate substations could be re-energized no earlier than 2:00 a.m. No later official customer count or restoration notice was found.
