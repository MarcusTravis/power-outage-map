Kyiv and nationwideUkraineRolling and emergency blackouts after Russian attacks on energy infrastructureUkrenergo said planned electricity outages would be in effect around the clock on Tuesday, Nov 17, in parts of Ukraine: capacity-restriction schedules for industry, and hourly outage schedules of 1 to 3 groups (queues) for all categories of consumers, because of the aftermath of attacks on the energy system. LB.ua, citing Ukrenergo, published that schedule update at 10:18 p.m. EET Monday. UNN, citing Ukrenergo in an 8:07 p.m. EET Monday report, said one group equals four hours without electricity, so Tuesday's scheduled outages may last from 4 to 12 hours; it said up-to-date schedules should be checked on each region's distribution-system operator. On Tuesday morning, new Russian attacks again caused widespread outages in the east and southeast. Ukraine's Energy Ministry, in a Tuesday Telegram statement reported by Interfax-Ukraine at 11:24 a.m. EET, said some consumers were left without power in the Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Zaporizhzhia, Mykolaiv, Odesa, Rivne, Sumy, Chernihiv, and Chernivtsi regions, and that the situation was extremely difficult in Dnipropetrovsk and Zaporizhzhia, where Russia has been continuously attacking energy facilities in the Dnipro region since last week; it said air-raid alerts were delaying repairs and that restored facilities were being attacked again. The ministry said emergency outages were being applied in the Dnipropetrovsk, Kirovohrad, Poltava, and Kharkiv regions because of equipment damage and reduced voltage on the high-voltage network, and that hourly outage schedules were in effect in most regions of the country, with capacity-restriction schedules for industry. It did not give customer counts for those regions. Ukrenergo, as reported by LB.ua at 10:14 a.m. EET Tuesday, said that after widespread power cuts on Tuesday morning, engineers were working to restore electricity in the Zaporizhzhia and Dnipropetrovsk regions, returning power first to critical infrastructure; it said crews had repaired equipment overnight, but new Russian attacks again caused damage and widespread outages, so restoration work had to start again before the previous repairs could be completed. Ukrenergo said emergency outages were in effect in the Poltava and Kharkiv regions as of Tuesday morning. DTEK said new Tuesday-morning strikes left 700,000 households without power in Dnipropetrovsk region (see the separate Dnipro row). In Zaporizhzhia and the region, Zaporizhzhiaoblenergo reported a mass outage around 8:00 a.m. EET Tuesday and said that, if there were no new attacks, it planned to fully restore supply in the city by 2:00 p.m. EET; it gave no customer count for the Tuesday outage (see the separate Zaporizhzhia row). Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said Russian strikes late Monday on the city's North and South bridges over the Dnipro, and on some other infrastructure, caused some water and power cuts, traffic restrictions, and partial damage to the subway; Reuters reported that account on Tuesday. No official customer count for the Kyiv power cuts was found. On Monday evening, Nov 16, Ukrenergo said enemy strikes had caused large-scale power outages in eastern Ukraine and that the situation was particularly difficult in Dnipro, Kharkiv, Poltava, and Zaporizhzhia. Mariupol, Donetsk, Volnovakha, Novoazovsk, and nearby citiesRussian-occupied Donetsk region, UkraineOccupation authorities said energy infrastructure was attacked on the nights of Nov 15 and Nov 16, causing an emergency in the regional power system. On Nov 15, the Russia-appointed head of the Donetsk occupation government, Andrey Chertkov, said the grid was damaged by a "powerful enemy attack," while some Russian media called it an accident. Vedomosti reported that Ukrainian forces struck energy infrastructure in the region overnight Nov 15Occupation authorities have not given a single independently confirmed total, and the latest official figures they issued still come from occupation sources. Donetsk Agency News (DAN), in an 8:30 p.m. local Nov 16 report, citing the occupation administration of the head and government of the occupied Donetsk region and Donetskenergo director Oleg Pavlyak, said 428,000 subscribers remained without electricity. Of those, it said 284,000 were de-energized because of the emergency shutdown of a power unit, and 144,000 household consumers were under a temporary capacity-limitation schedule (GVO) of 95 MW. It said the unit was expected to start by midnight, after which specialists planned to begin restoring supply to consumers in Donetsk, Mariupol, and the Novoazovsk, Volnovakha, and Novotroitsk districts. News-donetsk.ru, in an 8:14 a.m. local Tuesday, Nov 17 report citing the occupation Ministry of Coal and Energy, said energy workers had carried out the necessary restoration work and that most previously de-energized consumers had already been reconnected. It said the regional power system remained under strain, that because of a shortage of available capacity and high grid load, forced temporary outages were possible in Donetsk, Horlivka, and Yasynuvata, and that local outages were also occurring in Makiivka, Yenakiieve, Novoazovsk, and Shakhtarsk municipal districts. It said the outages were emergency in nature, were not planned in advance, and were introduced based on the current state of the grids and the load; it did not give a new customer count. RIA Novosti's Prime, in a Tuesday report citing the same ministry Telegram statement, likewise said most previously de-energized consumers had been reconnected and that the system remained under strain, without a new customer count. Earlier Monday morning figures, which have been superseded by the later Monday evening and Tuesday morning updates, included 201,220 subscribers without electricity in all districts of Donetsk, according to occupation-appointed mayor Alexey Kulemzin citing Donetskenergo, as reported by news-donetsk.ru at 10:55 a.m. local Nov 16, and more than 133,500 subscribers without electricity in Mariupol, with 941 transformer substations in the urban district still de-energized, according to the occupation administration of the Mariupol urban district as reported by MRPL.NEWS at 9:41 a.m. local Nov 16. These figures come from occupation authorities and were not independently confirmed in the sources reviewed.
