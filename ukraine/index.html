Dnipro and nearby districtsDnipropetrovsk, UkraineRussian attack on energy facilities in the regionNo official customer count. DTEK, in a Monday evening Telegram statement reported by Interfax-Ukraine at 6:59 p.m. EET and Ukrinform at 7:08 p.m. EET, said that after an attack on energy facilities in Dnipropetrovsk region, the city of Dnipro was completely without power, and that some residents of the Dniprovskyi, Samarivskyi, and Kamianskyi districts were also without electricity. DTEK said crews were already restoring supply, returning power first to water and heating systems and social facilities, and warned that further Russian attacks could slow the work; it gave no restoration time. Dnipro Mayor Borys Filatov, in a Monday Telegram post reported by NV at 7:10 p.m. EET, said that after Russian attacks on the city, almost half of Dnipro was without electricity, and he called it the sixth blackout in two weeks. He also said two people were killed, eight were in hospital, and 10 apartment buildings were damaged. Filatov said Russia had attacked Dnipro with drones from the morning of Nov 16. Ukrenergo, as reported by Ukrinform at 7:30 p.m. EET, said the situation was particularly difficult in Dnipro, Kharkiv, Poltava, and Zaporizhzhia, where electricity supply was severely restricted because of significant damage to energy infrastructure, and that restoration work was continuing around the clock, with priority for hospitals, water and heating, and other critical facilities. Telegraf, in a 9:16 p.m. EET Monday report updated at 9:20 p.m. EET, said the metro had returned to scheduled service closer to evening after traction substations lost power, while ground electric transport was only partly restored, with some tram and trolleybus routes still not running. Northern Odesa regionOdesa, UkraineRussian attack on DTEK energy facilities52,200 households without power, according to a Nov 16 statement by DTEK Odeski Elektromerezhi. DTEK said a Russian attack on the morning of Nov 16 hit its energy facilities in the north of Odesa region, that 52,200 households were temporarily left without electricity, that specialists were inspecting surviving equipment in order to begin repairs as soon as possible, and that all work is being carried out under safety rules, including during air-raid alerts.
