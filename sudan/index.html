Drop Site News, in a Nov 20, 2026 report from Khartoum, said a particularly intense wave of drone strikes at the end of October and into November targeting power stations, fuel supplies, and electricity infrastructure had brought the country's power grid to the brink of collapse, and that two weeks after the wave of attacks, major substations remained out of service. Shaker Ismail, an engineer with the Sudanese Electricity Company, told Drop Site that repairing some of the country's most important power facilities could take months or longer, that high-capacity transformers and digital control systems had been damaged, and that replacement transformers must be manufactured to order abroad. Ismail said the Um Dabakir thermal power plant in White Nile State was badly damaged in an Oct 29 strike and forced to shut down, and that the Al-Markhiyat substation west of Omdurman was put out of service after an Oct 27 strike; he said affected areas continued to experience prolonged outages and scheduled power cuts as of this week. The report said the Sudan Electricity Holding Company and Ministry of Energy had yet to publicly announce the restoration of the major facilities damaged in the attacks. A United Nations report published Nov 17, issued in Geneva and posted on the UN in Sudan website, said that in just over two weeks between Oct 20 and Nov 4, 29 drone strikes, attributed to the Rapid Support Forces, hit electricity facilities and fuel stations across 11 states, triggering widespread power blackouts and further fuel shortages affecting millions far from the conflict frontlines. An earlier Al Jazeera Nov 15 feature said there is no clear schedule for the resulting power cuts, which can last anywhere between six hours and a day, and, citing The Associated Press, said repeated drone attacks on power stations have seen electricity generation fall from about 4,400 megawatts before the war to about 1,100 MW. Residents and a small-business owner in Port Sudan described prolonged, unreliable cuts, including outages lasting more than 24 hours.
