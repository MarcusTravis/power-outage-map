Khartoum, Port Sudan, and nationwideSudanDrone attacks on power stations and other electricity infrastructure during the war between the Rapid Support Forces and the Sudanese Armed ForcesNo official customer count. Al Jazeera, in a Nov 15 report, said the RSF has intensified attacks on power stations around Khartoum state over the past month, creating an energy crisis in the capital, and that there is no clear schedule for the resulting power cuts, which can last anywhere between six hours and a day. It said that between Oct 20 and Nov 2, 2026, there were at least 18 attacks on electricity infrastructure across at least nine states, leading to declining power generation capacity and fuel shortages. Citing The Associated Press, it said repeated drone attacks on power stations have seen electricity generation fall from about 4,400 megawatts before the war to about 1,100 MW today. Residents and a small-business owner in Port Sudan described prolonged, unreliable cuts, including outages lasting more than 24 hours. The UN resident and humanitarian coordinator, Denise Brown, was quoted warning that repeated strikes on power and fuel stations were disrupting dialysis, hospital births, and operations. No later official national customer count or restoration timetable was found.——aljazeera.comNov 15, 2026 (Al Jazeera feature, published Nov 15); generation figure attributed to The Associated Press; rechecked 6:05 PM CT Monday, no later official national customer count or restoration timetable found
