The collapse of tower T313, detected at 6:04 a.m. Thursday, severed two transmission circuits linking Sabah's west and east coasts, according to Sabah Electricity, as reported by The Star, Malay Mail, H Metro, and Sinar Daily. Sabah Electricity's chief executive officer, Datuk Mohd Yaakob Jaafar, as reported by The Star at 8:48 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time, said the tower's foundation along the banks of the Liwagu River was washed away following persistent heavy rains, resulting in the collapse of the tower, which is part of the grid that transmits 295 megawatts of electricity to the east coast. After the collapse, east-coast supply has been coming from existing generation in Sandakan and Tawau, supported by mobile generators and a battery energy storage system, with rotational load shedding because that local capacity is not enough to meet demand, according to Sabah Electricity accounts reported Friday by H Metro, The Star, and Sinar Daily.Sabah Electricity, as reported by The Star at 6:01 p.m. Saturday, NovSabah Electricity, as reported by The Star at 6:01 p.m. Saturday, Nov 28, Malaysia time (4:01 a.m. CT Saturday), said in a progress update on its Facebook page that its technical team had completed installation of the main temporary Emergency Restoration System (ERS) tower structure at 1:45 p.m. Saturday, Nov 28, Malaysia time (11:45 p.m. CT Friday, Nov 27), and would next install the tower's crossarms and supporting components in preparation for installing transmission cables. The same report said the collapse had affected 153,599 registered consumers and that full supply restoration was targeted by Dec 3 at the latest. Daily Express, in a Saturday, Nov 28, report citing Bernama and Sabah Electricity Chief Operating Officer Mohd Riza Zalmee Mohd Ghazali speaking at the site in Kampung Sagindai Lama, said electricity supply in several districts along Sabah's east coast had been restored to 60 percent as of 1:00 p.m. Saturday, Nov 28, Malaysia time (11:00 p.m. CT Friday, Nov 27). The same account said the Thursday 6:04 a.m. collapse of tower T313 had severed two transmission-line circuits, disrupting supply in eight districts and affecting 153,599 registered consumers; that the east-coast transmission network was restored by 6:00 p.m. on the day of the incident and generation units in the region resumed full operation by 8:00 p.m. that evening; but that rotational power rationing continued because of a shortfall in available generation capacity, since nearly half of east-coast supply had been drawn from the west coast. The same report quoted Mohd Riza Zalmee as saying the company was striving to complete restoration ahead of the initial timeline, and that based on preliminary assessments and current progress, and provided the weather remained favorable, it was aiming to complete recovery earlier, by Dec 1; the report said Sabah Electricity had initially targeted full restoration by Dec 2. Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim, as reported by Malay Mail, first published at 4:10 p.m. Saturday, Nov 28, Malaysia time (2:10 a.m. CT Saturday), called on Sabah Electricity to restore supply earlier than scheduled and to give priority to temporary electricity for schools, examination centres, and critical public facilities, and said the disruption had hit up to eight districts after the Ranau tower collapse, affecting 153,599 consumers. Sabah Chief Minister Datuk Seri Hajiji Noor, as reported by Malay Mail, first published at 7:12 p.m. Saturday, Nov 28, Malaysia time (5:12 a.m. CT Saturday), citing Bernama, said he had been briefed by Sabah Electricity, that the tower repairs were expected to be completed within six days, that reserve power sources were being used, and that the situation had improved, although some areas might still experience minor supply shortages.
