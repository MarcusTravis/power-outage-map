The company said the incident was detected at 6:04 a.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time. Daily Express, in a Thursday update, said the collapse of tower T313 at 6:04 a.m. disrupted two electricity circuits supplying the East Coast and forced the closure of the main Ranau-Telupid road in both directions. The Star, in a later Thursday report, said the cause of the tower collapse had yet to be officially announced. Sayang Sabah, citing a Sabah Electricity statement published at 5:59 p.m. Thursday, said rotational electricity rationing was being implemented on the east coast because, after the T313 incident, supply there depended on limited local generation capacityThe Star, in a report dated 7:15 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia timeThe Star, in a report dated 7:15 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time (5:15 a.m. CT Thursday), said eight districts -- Sandakan, Tawau, Lahad Datu, Semporna, Kunak, Beluran, Telupid, and Kinabatangan -- were affected by the outage that began at 6:04 a.m., that Sabah Electricity had been taking various steps, including the islanding method, to speed up restoration, that electricity supply returned to parts of Tawau first, followed by parts of Sandakan, Semporna, and Telupid by afternoon, and that efforts were still under way to restore power to the remaining areas. The same report said the cause of the tower collapse and the estimated time for full restoration had yet to be officially announced. It also described disruptions to businesses, unpowered traffic lights, and mobile coverage after backup power at some telecommunications towers ran out, and said the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission issued an emergency alert advising residents in affected areas to limit mobile-phone use to essential communications. The Star said Thursday's incident came just over a year after a Sept 13, 2025, east-coast blackout that affected nearly 230,000 consumers; that earlier figure was not given as a count for Thursday's outage. Sayang Sabah, in a report published at 5:59 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time (3:59 a.m. CT Thursday), citing a Sabah Electricity statement, said rotational electricity rationing was being implemented on the east coast after the T313 transmission-tower incident, because supply there depended on limited local generation capacity, and that the rationing was needed so that more customers could receive supply and to keep the system stable and avoid a wider disruption. The same statement said mobile generator sets were being moved to important locations including hospitals, schools, and police stations, that the company was using all available generation resources to increase the supply that could be delivered, and that the latest information on the rationing and the areas involved would be issued through the official Sabah Electricity Careline Facebook page. Daily Express, in a Thursday update, said Sabah Electricity had activated its operations room and deployed technical teams to restore power in stages across Sandakan, Tawau, and Lahad Datu, that mobile generator sets were being deployed, and that kidney dialysis treatments at the Duchess of Kent Hospital Haemodialysis Unit proceeded as normal using generator sets, which continued to power the hospital's main buildings.
