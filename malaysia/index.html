Sandakan, Tawau, Lahad Datu, Semporna, Kunak, Beluran, Telupid, and KinabatanganSabah, MalaysiaA collapse of a 275kV transmission tower in Ranau early ThursdayA collapse of a 275kV transmission tower in Ranau early Thursday morning, Nov 26, 2026, which Sabah Electricity said affected the west-to-east-coast transmission grid. The company's chief executive officer, Datuk Mohd Yaakob Jaafar, as reported by The Star at 8:48 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time, said the tower's foundation along the banks of the Liwagu River was washed away following persistent heavy rains, resulting in the collapse of the tower, which is part of the grid that transmits 295 megawatts of electricity to the east coast. He said only the collapsed tower No. 313 needed to be replaced, that other nearby towers in Liwagu were not affected, that riverbank erosion had been detected during a routine aerial inspection in January and that rock and sand embankments had been constructed in March, and that existing generation capacity was insufficient to meet the area's overall demand. Borneo Post, in a report dated 9:25 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Sabah time (7:25 a.m. CT Thursday), quoted Yaakob as saying the 275kV Kolopis-Segaliud Line 1 and Line 2 tripped after the collapse of a transmission tower due to riverbank erosion along the Liwagu River. The Star, in a report dated 11:08 a.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (9:08 p.m. CT Thursday), quoting Sandakan Municipal Council president Datuk Walter Kenson, said the collapse was believed to have been caused by ground movement and continuous heavy rain and affected the 275kV Kolopis-Segaliud transmission line. Earlier Thursday accounts, including Daily Express and The Star, had identified the collapsed tower as T313 and said the incident, detected at 6:04 a.m. Thursday, disrupted two circuits and forced the closure of the main Ranau-Telupid or Ranau-Sandakan road. One passage in The Star's 8:48 p.m. report also referred to the tower as T133.Sabah Electricity, in a Friday, Nov 27 statement reported by MalaySabah Electricity, in a Friday, Nov 27 statement reported by Malay Mail, first published at 1:38 p.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (11:38 p.m. CT Thursday, Nov 26) and last modified at 1:45 p.m. Friday (11:45 p.m. CT Thursday), said it is rationing power in eastern Sabah through coordinated feeder rotation schedules after transmission tower T313 collapsed in Ranau on Thursday, and that four districts were affected as of 10:00 a.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (8:00 p.m. CT Thursday): Sandakan, Tawau, Semporna, and Lahad Datu. A spokesperson said the utility is preparing a full rationing schedule, including the times involved, to be announced later. The same report said the collapse of tower T313, detected at 6:04 a.m. Thursday, severed two transmission circuits from Sabah's west coast to the east coast, disrupting electricity supply in eight districts, affecting 153,599 registered consumers. The Star, in a report dated 11:08 a.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (9:08 p.m. CT Thursday), quoted Sandakan Municipal Council president Datuk Walter Kenson, who is also Sandakan disaster management committee chairman, as saying that while efforts were under way to restore electricity supply through local power stations, their generation capacity was insufficient to meet the district's overall demand, and that power rationing in several areas in Sandakan was unavoidable. He said the district experienced a prolonged power outage from about 6:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m. on Thursday, Nov 26, that Sabah Electricity was relying on power stations in Libaran, Batu Sapi, Labuk, Seguntor, and Mowtas to restore supply in Sandakan, and that the unstable electricity supply had also affected water-treatment-plant operations, forcing water rationing in several areas. Earlier, Sabah Electricity Chief Executive Officer Datuk Mohd Yaakob Jaafar, as reported by The Star at 8:48 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time (6:48 a.m. CT Thursday), said the company had restored limited power generation in the state's east coast after the collapse, and that they expect to restore normal power supply to 153,000 consumers latest by Dec 3. He said they had restored limited power in the seven affected districts and that electricity supply capacity on the east coast would remain limited until the transmission lines were fully restored. He said Sabah Electricity would implement rotational power rationing in different areas starting immediately and would announce the schedules. The same Thursday evening report said repair teams and emergency transmission towers had been deployed to the site, and that eight generator sets had been mobilized, prioritizing critical facilities such as hospitals, schools, water treatment plants, and police stations. The districts named in that evening account were Sandakan, Lahad Datu, Tawau, Semporna, Kinabatangan, Kunak, and Beluran. Earlier Thursday accounts, including The Star at 7:15 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time (5:15 a.m. CT Thursday), had said eight districts — Sandakan, Tawau, Lahad Datu, Semporna, Kunak, Beluran, Telupid, and Kinabatangan — were affected by the outage that began at 6:04 a.m., that electricity had returned to parts of Tawau first, followed by parts of Sandakan, Semporna, and Telupid by afternoon, and that restoration to remaining areas was still under way.6:04 a.m. Thursday, Nov 26, 2026, Malaysia time (4:04 p.m. CT6:04 a.m. Thursday, Nov 26, 2026, Malaysia time (4:04 p.m. CT Wednesday, Nov 25), according to Sabah Electricity, as reported by The Star, Malay Mail, and Sinar HarianSabah Electricity, in the Friday, Nov 27 statement reported by MalaySabah Electricity, in the Friday, Nov 27 statement reported by Malay Mail, said it was preparing a full rationing schedule, including the times involved, to be announced later, and did not give a new official date in that statement for a return to normal supply.
