The collapse of tower T313, detected at 6:04 a.m. Thursday, severed two transmission circuits linking Sabah's west and east coasts, according to Sabah Electricity, as reported by The Star, Malay Mail, H Metro, and Sinar Daily. Sabah Electricity's chief executive officer, Datuk Mohd Yaakob Jaafar, as reported by The Star at 8:48 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time, said the tower's foundation along the banks of the Liwagu River was washed away following persistent heavy rains, resulting in the collapse of the tower, which is part of the grid that transmits 295 megawatts of electricity to the east coast. After the collapse, east-coast supply has been coming from existing generation in Sandakan and Tawau, supported by mobile generators and a battery energy storage system, with rotational load shedding because that local capacity is not enough to meet demand, according to Sabah Electricity accounts reported Friday by H Metro, The Star, and Sinar Daily.Sabah Electricity, as reported by Astro Awani citing Bernama at 1:15Sabah Electricity, as reported by Astro Awani citing Bernama at 1:15 p.m. Saturday, Nov 28, Malaysia time (11:15 p.m. CT Friday, Nov 27), said it had added staff at its call-management center, which was operating 24 hours, after the east-coast electricity disruption, and recorded its appreciation for the patience of customers, especially the 153,599 registered consumers affected on Sabah's east coast. The same Saturday account said Sabah Electricity was targeting restoration of the electricity supply on Dec 2 or earlier, and that the collapse of tower T313 because of unstable soil at 6:04 a.m. Thursday had severed two transmission-line circuits from the west coast to the east coast, disrupting supply in eight districts. Sarawak Daily, in a Nov 28 report citing a Sabah Electricity statement reported by Bernama, said the utility's technical team continued installing a temporary transmission tower on the night of Nov 27 after completing the anchors and tower base at 6:28 p.m. that day, that the work was the second phase of an Emergency Restoration System (ERS) installation in Ranau, and that the next stage was erecting the upper section of the tower and stringing cables once the structure had been confirmed stable. The same account said Sabah Electricity said restoration work was proceeding according to its planned timeline, with personnel safety remaining the priority, and that the company aims to restore power by Dec 2 or earlier.
