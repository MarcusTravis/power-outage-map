Sandakan, Tawau, Lahad Datu, Semporna, Kunak, Beluran, Telupid, and KinabatanganSabah, MalaysiaA collapse of a 275kV transmission tower in Ranau early ThursdayA collapse of a 275kV transmission tower in Ranau early Thursday morning, Nov 26, 2026, which Sabah Electricity said affected the west-to-east-coast transmission grid. The collapse of tower T313, detected at 6:04 a.m. Thursday, severed two transmission circuits linking Sabah's west and east coasts, according to Sabah Electricity, as reported by The Star, Malay Mail, H Metro, and Sinar Daily. Sabah Electricity's chief executive officer, Datuk Mohd Yaakob Jaafar, as reported by The Star at 8:48 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time, said the tower's foundation along the banks of the Liwagu River was washed away following persistent heavy rains, resulting in the collapse of the tower, which is part of the grid that transmits 295 megawatts of electricity to the east coast. After the collapse, east-coast supply has been coming from existing generation in Sandakan and Tawau, supported by mobile generators and a battery energy storage system, with rotational load shedding because that local capacity is not enough to meet demand, according to Sabah Electricity accounts reported Friday by H Metro, The Star, and Sinar Daily.Sabah Electricity, as reported by H Metro, citing a company FacebookSabah Electricity, as reported by H Metro, citing a company Facebook video update on the outage, said that as of 3:00 p.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (1:00 a.m. CT Friday), power-supply restoration in several east-coast districts had reached between 23 and 57 percent: Beluran 57 percent, Tawau 56 percent, Semporna 50 percent, Sandakan 45 percent, Kinabatangan 36 percent, Kunak 33 percent, and Lahad Datu 23 percent. The same account said restoration was being carried out through the Sandakan and Tawau power stations and a battery energy storage system (BESS), and that rotational load shedding was still required in some areas because existing supply capacity could not yet meet current demand. The Star, in a report dated 4:24 p.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (2:24 a.m. CT Friday), said that under a rotational power-supply schedule released at 2:00 p.m. Friday, affected areas across seven districts — Sandakan, Tawau, Lahad Datu, Semporna, Kinabatangan, Beluran, and Kunak — had been divided into four groups, with rotations scheduled up to Monday, Nov 30, that each rotation was expected to receive electricity for three hours, and that the first rotation was to begin at 3:00 p.m. Friday. The same report said the estimated supply periods were based on the current condition of power-generation systems in Tawau and Sandakan, with actual timing and duration subject to grid conditions, and that the affected feeders served residential, commercial, and industrial areas as well as essential facilities, including hospitals, airports, health clinics, and water treatment plants. Sinar Daily, in a report dated 4:41 p.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (2:41 a.m. CT Friday), likewise described the four-group, three-hour rotation schedule running from Friday through Nov 30 in those seven districts, and published group-by-group time slots and named feeders and locations. The Thursday collapse, detected at 6:04 a.m., disrupted electricity supply across eight districts and affected 153,599 registered consumers, according to Sabah Electricity, as reported by The Star, Malay Mail, H Metro, and Sinar Daily. Telupid was named among the eight districts affected by the Thursday collapse and was not named among the seven districts listed in the Friday rotation schedule. Malay Mail, first published at 1:38 p.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (11:38 p.m. CT Thursday, Nov 26), had earlier reported a Friday statement that four districts — Sandakan, Tawau, Semporna, and Lahad Datu — were affected as of 10:00 a.m. Friday by coordinated feeder-rotation rationing, with a full rationing schedule then still to be announced.6:04 a.m. Thursday, Nov 26, 2026, Malaysia time (4:04 p.m. CT6:04 a.m. Thursday, Nov 26, 2026, Malaysia time (4:04 p.m. CT Wednesday, Nov 25), according to Sabah Electricity, as reported by The Star, Malay Mail, and Sinar HarianSabah Electricity, as reported by The Star at 4:24 p.m. Friday, Nov 27,Sabah Electricity, as reported by The Star at 4:24 p.m. Friday, Nov 27, Malaysia time (2:24 a.m. CT Friday), said a rotational power-supply schedule released at 2:00 p.m. Friday divided affected areas across seven districts into four groups, with rotations scheduled up to Monday, Nov 30, and that each rotation was expected to receive electricity for three hours, with actual timing and duration subject to grid conditions.
