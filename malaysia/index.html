Sandakan, Tawau, Lahad Datu, Semporna, Kunak, Beluran, Telupid, and KinabatanganSabah, MalaysiaA collapse of a 275kV transmission tower in Ranau early ThursdayA collapse of a 275kV transmission tower in Ranau early Thursday morning, Nov 26, 2026, which Sabah Electricity said affected the west-to-east-coast transmission grid. The company's chief executive officer, Datuk Mohd Yaakob Jaafar, as reported by The Star at 8:48 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time, said the tower's foundation along the banks of the Liwagu River was washed away following persistent heavy rains, resulting in the collapse of the tower, which is part of the grid that transmits 295 megawatts of electricity to the east coast. He said only the collapsed tower No. 313 needed to be replaced, that other nearby towers in Liwagu were not affected, that riverbank erosion had been detected during a routine aerial inspection in January and that rock and sand embankments had been constructed in March, and that existing generation capacity was insufficient to meet the area's overall demand. Earlier Thursday accounts, including Daily Express and The Star, had identified the collapsed tower as T313 and said the incident, detected at 6:04 a.m. Thursday, disrupted two circuits and forced the closure of the main Ranau-Telupid or Ranau-Sandakan road. One passage in The Star's 8:48 p.m. report also referred to the tower as T133.Sabah Electricity Chief Executive Officer Datuk Mohd Yaakob Jaafar, asSabah Electricity Chief Executive Officer Datuk Mohd Yaakob Jaafar, as reported by The Star at 8:48 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time (6:48 a.m. CT Thursday), said the company had restored limited power generation in the state's east coast after the collapse of the transmission tower in Ranau, and that they expect to restore normal power supply to 153,000 consumers latest by Dec 3. He said they had restored limited power in the seven affected districts and that electricity supply capacity on the east coast would remain limited until the transmission lines were fully restored. He said Sabah Electricity would implement rotational power rationing in different areas starting immediately and would announce the schedules. The same report said repair teams and emergency transmission towers had been deployed to the site, and that eight generator sets had been mobilized, prioritizing critical facilities such as hospitals, schools, water treatment plants, and police stations. The districts named in that evening account were Sandakan, Lahad Datu, Tawau, Semporna, Kinabatangan, Kunak, and Beluran. Earlier Thursday accounts, including The Star at 7:15 p.m. Thursday, Nov 26, Malaysia time (5:15 a.m. CT Thursday), had said eight districts — Sandakan, Tawau, Lahad Datu, Semporna, Kunak, Beluran, Telupid, and Kinabatangan — were affected by the outage that began at 6:04 a.m., that electricity had returned to parts of Tawau first, followed by parts of Sandakan, Semporna, and Telupid by afternoon, and that restoration to remaining areas was still under way. Sabah Electricity, as reported earlier Thursday by Sayang Sabah at 5:59 p.m. Thursday (3:59 a.m. CT Thursday), had already said rotational rationing was being implemented because, after the tower incident, supply on the east coast depended on limited local generation capacity, and that mobile generator sets were being moved to hospitals, schools, and police stations. Daily Express, in a Thursday update, said kidney dialysis treatments at the Duchess of Kent Hospital Haemodialysis Unit proceeded as normal using generator sets, which continued to power the hospital's main buildings.
