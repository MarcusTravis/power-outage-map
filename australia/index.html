Eyre PeninsulaSouth Australia, AustraliaStorms knocked down two major transmission towers and damaged a thirdStorms knocked down two major transmission towers and damaged a third at Wharminda on ElectraNet's 132 kV line between Cultana and Port Lincoln, cutting transmission supply south and west of WhyallaABC News, in an article posted at 5:59 p.m. ACDT Thursday, Nov 19, andABC News, in an article posted at 5:59 p.m. ACDT Thursday, Nov 19, and updated at 10:33 p.m. ACDT Thursday (6:03 a.m. CT), said more than 20,000 properties lost power on South Australia's Eyre Peninsula after a significant storm cell knocked over transmission towers in the region. It said about 24,000 properties on the peninsula lost power after two major transmission towers were downed, and a third was damaged, at Wharminda, and that by 10:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (5:30 a.m. CT) the number of properties without power had fallen to about 14,500. ABC said that as of 7:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (2:30 a.m. CT), almost 29,000 properties across the state were without electricity, about 24,000 of them on Eyre Peninsula, where supply was cut during lightning storms that afternoon, and that the same wild weather later started to hit Adelaide, causing more than 3,000 properties to lose power because of suspected storm-related faults on the distribution network. Earlier, ABC reported that as of 4:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday, SA Power Networks said extensive outages were impacting about 24,000 customers on Eyre Peninsula and western parts of South Australia. That 10:00 p.m. figure of about 14,500 properties is the latest remaining count found. A midday Thursday recheck at 12:05 PM CT Thursday found no later official remaining customer count. WattClarity, citing an ElectraNet statement, said that at approximately 3:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday severe storms brought down multiple transmission towers on ElectraNet's network, interrupting supply to tens of thousands of customers south and west of Whyalla. In a statement issued about 6:30 p.m. ACDT Thursday (2:00 a.m. CT), ElectraNet said the outage extended south and west of Whyalla, that power was unlikely to be restored in the immediate term, and that its first step was to safely assess the damage and then determine the quickest and safest way to restore services. SA Power Networks, in a 6:45 p.m. ACDT Thursday update (2:15 a.m. CT), said a severe storm had brought down two ElectraNet towers on Eyre Peninsula, that the damage was significant, and that restoring power safely may take some time. The South Australian Department for Energy and Mining said severe weather had caused significant damage to electricity transmission infrastructure, including the destruction of two transmission towers on the 132 kV line between Cultana and Port Lincoln, disrupting supply to communities across the central and lower Eyre Peninsula and West Coast. It said the Minister for Energy and Mining had declared an electricity supply emergency under the Emergency Management Act 2004, that the declaration took effect at 6:15 p.m. ACDT Thursday (1:45 a.m. CT), and that it will remain in force for 14 days unless revoked earlier. After a meeting of SA cabinet's emergency management committee, Premier Peter Malinauskas said Minister Tom Koutsantonis had signed an emergency declaration authorizing him to instruct the Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO) to seek spare generation, and that generators in and around Port Lincoln were going through a process of being energized. Malinauskas said such a step had the potential to restore electricity to the vast majority of those then without it, but that success was not guaranteed, and he said several schools in the region would stay closed the next day. Acting Police Commissioner Linda Williams said the state emergency centre had been activated at 6:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (1:30 a.m. CT). Malinauskas said eight hospitals across Eyre Peninsula and Ceduna on the West Coast had been affected and were operating on generators. AEMO said that at that stage there was no broader impact to power system security and reliability in the National Electricity Market. WattClarity, in a Thursday-evening article, reported AEMO Market Notice 146498, issued at 8:42 p.m. NEM time Thursday, Nov 19 (8:12 p.m. ACDT Thursday, 3:42 a.m. CT). The notice said that under section 27C of the Emergency Management Act 2004 (SA), AEMO had been directed to issue a direction under the National Electricity Rules to Synergen Power Pty Limited for the Port Lincoln generating unit POR01, requiring it to synchronise and maintain the local load and manage frequency control in the island from 8:35 p.m. NEM time Thursday (8:05 p.m. ACDT, 3:35 a.m. CT) until further notice, citing a declared electricity supply emergency under section 27B(1) of the Act. WattClarity said that notice implied that part of the lower Eyre Peninsula around Port Lincoln was then running as its own islanded system. That direction is not an official remaining customer count and is not a confirmation that supply had been restored to all affected customers.
