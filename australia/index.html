Eyre PeninsulaSouth Australia, AustraliaStorms knocked down two major transmission towers and damaged a thirdStorms knocked down two major transmission towers and damaged a third at Wharminda, cutting ElectraNet transmission supply south and west of WhyallaABC News, in an article posted at 5:59 p.m. ACDT Thursday, Nov 19, andABC News, in an article posted at 5:59 p.m. ACDT Thursday, Nov 19, and updated at 10:33 p.m. ACDT Thursday (6:03 a.m. CT), said more than 20,000 properties lost power on South Australia's Eyre Peninsula after a significant storm cell knocked over transmission towers in the region. It said about 24,000 properties on the peninsula remained without power at 9:30 p.m. ACDT Thursday (5:00 a.m. CT) after two major transmission towers were downed, and a third was damaged, at Wharminda, and that by 10:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (5:30 a.m. CT) the number of properties impacted had fallen to about 14,500. ABC said that as of 7:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (2:30 a.m. CT), almost 29,000 properties across the state were without electricity, about 24,000 of them on Eyre Peninsula, where supply was cut during lightning storms that afternoon, and that the same wild weather later started to hit Adelaide, causing more than 3,000 properties to lose power because of suspected storm-related faults on the distribution network. Earlier, ABC reported that as of 4:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday, SA Power Networks said extensive outages were impacting about 24,000 customers on Eyre Peninsula and western parts of South Australia. In a statement issued about 6:30 p.m. ACDT Thursday (2:00 a.m. CT), ElectraNet said the outage extended south and west of Whyalla, that power was unlikely to be restored in the immediate term, and that its first step was to safely assess the damage and then determine the quickest and safest way to restore services. The SA government warned that the main electricity transmission line to the Eyre Peninsula could take days to repair. After a meeting of SA cabinet's emergency management committee, Premier Peter Malinauskas said Minister Tom Koutsantonis had signed an emergency declaration authorizing him to instruct the Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO) to seek spare generation, and that generators in and around Port Lincoln were going through a process of being energized. Malinauskas said such a step had the potential to restore electricity to the vast majority of those then without it, but that success was not guaranteed, and he said several schools in the region would stay closed the next day. Acting Police Commissioner Linda Williams said the state emergency centre had been activated at 6:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (1:30 a.m. CT). Malinauskas said eight hospitals across Eyre Peninsula and Ceduna on the West Coast had been affected and were operating on generators. AEMO said that at that stage there was no broader impact to power system security and reliability in the National Electricity Market.
