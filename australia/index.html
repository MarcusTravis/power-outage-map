Eyre Peninsula and West CoastSouth Australia, AustraliaStorms knocked down two major transmission towers and damaged a thirdStorms knocked down two major transmission towers and damaged a third at Wharminda on ElectraNet's 132 kV line between Cultana and Port Lincoln, cutting transmission supply south and west of Whyalla. ElectraNet executive network manager Alex Trento said Friday the towers were built to withstand a one-in-2,000-year weather event and that the localized weather appeared to have been even stronger than the winds they were designed for. In a later Friday-afternoon ABC News report, Trento said the towers that fell were designed to withstand up to around 170 km/h wind speed and were also tested to destruction to their design standard, that he did not know what the wind speed was when the towers toppled but that the probability of it occurring was "very rare," and that a structural fault in the towers was "a possibility" and ElectraNet was investigating. He said that, seeing the localisation of the event, ElectraNet still thought the towers had been designed and constructed effectively.ABC News, in an article posted at 4:08 p.m. ACDT Friday, Nov 20, andABC News, in an article posted at 4:08 p.m. ACDT Friday, Nov 20, and updated at 4:27 p.m. ACDT Friday (11:57 p.m. CT Thursday, Nov 19), said that while a large portion of the region had had power restored, less than 2,000 properties remained without power as at 3:30 p.m. ACDT Friday (11:00 p.m. CT Thursday), according to SA Power Networks's outage map. Earlier Friday morning, the same live blog, citing the SA Power Networks website, said that as of 9:00 a.m. ACDT Friday (4:30 p.m. CT Thursday) there were about 10,000 properties without power across South Australia, down from a peak of around 30,000 Thursday evening, but the bulk of the 10,000 were on Eyre Peninsula, which has been islanded from the national energy market. ABC said about 24,000 properties on the peninsula were cut off on Thursday when the storm downed the towers. The Friday-afternoon article likewise said the storm cut power to about 24,000 properties at 3 p.m. on Thursday. ABC, citing ElectraNet, had said about 10,000 customers in Port Lincoln and surrounding towns had got their power back, and that ElectraNet reported that power was restored to the Port Lincoln area using local generators at 9:45 p.m. ACDT Thursday, Nov 19 (5:15 a.m. CT Thursday). InDaily, in a Friday morning Nov 20 report, likewise said that in a Friday-morning update ElectraNet said local generators restored power to about 10,000 customers in Port Lincoln and surrounding towns about 9:45 p.m. on Thursday. The Friday-afternoon ABC article said a 20-megawatt generator located in Port Lincoln had been powering the township and lower Eyre Peninsula since Thursday night, and that all residents in the area had been urged to turn off their solar panels so the generator would not exceed its level of demand. Premier Peter Malinauskas, as quoted in that article, said the generator was working as it should but that "we're not out of the woods yet," citing further storm events, extreme fire conditions over the weekend, and the risk that the aging Port Lincoln generator might fail. SA Power Networks, in an 8:30 a.m. Friday, Nov 20 update (4:00 p.m. CT Thursday) on its Eyre Peninsula outages page, said it was seeking the support of customers on Eyre Peninsula who had power restored overnight, that those customers were being supplied by temporary generation while ElectraNet continued to repair its network, and that customers with rooftop solar that exports to the network should switch off their inverters and leave them off until SA Power Networks advised them they could turn them back on. The South Australian Department for Energy and Mining said severe weather had caused significant damage to electricity transmission infrastructure, including the destruction of two transmission towers on the 132 kV line between Cultana and Port Lincoln, disrupting supply to communities across the central and lower Eyre Peninsula and West Coast. It said the Minister for Energy and Mining had declared an electricity supply emergency under the Emergency Management Act 2004, that the declaration took effect at 6:15 p.m. ACDT Thursday (1:45 a.m. CT), and that it will remain in force for 14 days unless revoked earlier. ABC, citing the Department for Education in South Australia, had said some schools had opened in Port Lincoln on Friday after power was restored to parts of the area, but that more than 30 sites remained closed as of 7:30 a.m. ACDT Friday (3:00 p.m. CT Thursday), including schools in Cleve, Lock, Penong, and Streaky Bay. Earlier Thursday figures, from an ABC News article posted at 5:59 p.m. ACDT Thursday, Nov 19, and updated at 10:33 p.m. ACDT Thursday (6:03 a.m. CT), said about 24,000 properties on the peninsula lost power after two major transmission towers were downed and a third was damaged at Wharminda, and that by 10:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (5:30 a.m. CT) the number of properties without power had fallen to about 14,500. ABC said that as of 7:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (2:30 a.m. CT), almost 29,000 properties across the state were without electricity, about 24,000 of them on Eyre Peninsula, and that the same wild weather later started to hit Adelaide, causing more than 3,000 properties to lose power because of suspected storm-related faults on the distribution network. Malinauskas said Thursday that eight hospitals across Eyre Peninsula and Ceduna on the West Coast had been affected and were operating on generators; InDaily, in its Friday report, quoted him as saying all of the hospitals' backup power systems had worked appropriately.
