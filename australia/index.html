ElectraNet executive network manager Alex Trento said Friday the towers were built to withstand a one-in-2,000-year weather event and that the localized weather appeared to have been even stronger than the winds they were designed forABC News, in an article posted at 9:31 a.m. ACDT Friday, Nov 20, andABC News, in an article posted at 9:31 a.m. ACDT Friday, Nov 20, and updated at 10:37 a.m. ACDT Friday (6:07 p.m. CT Thursday, Nov 19), said swathes of South Australia's Eyre Peninsula remained without power after a storm knocked down major electricity transmission towers. ABC, citing ElectraNet, said about 10,000 customers in Port Lincoln and surrounding towns had got their power back. ABC also said ElectraNet reported that power was restored to the Port Lincoln area using local generators at 9:45 p.m. ACDT Thursday, Nov 19 (5:15 a.m. CT Thursday). InDaily, in a Friday morning Nov 20 report, likewise said that in a Friday-morning update ElectraNet said local generators restored power to about 10,000 customers in Port Lincoln and surrounding towns about 9:45 p.m. on Thursday. According to the SA Power Networks website, ABC said, there were as of 9:00 a.m. ACDT Friday (4:30 p.m. CT Thursday) about 10,000 properties without power across South Australia, down from a peak of around 30,000 Thursday evening, but the bulk of the 10,000 were on Eyre Peninsula, which has been islanded from the national energy market. That 9:00 a.m. Friday statewide figure of about 10,000 properties, with the bulk of them on Eyre Peninsula, is the latest remaining count found. ABC said about 24,000 properties on the peninsula were cut off on Thursday when the storm downed the towers, and that while supply had since been restored to many of those properties, thousands were still without power Friday morning, especially on northern parts of the peninsula. ABC, citing the Department for Education in South Australia, said some schools had opened in Port Lincoln on Friday after power was restored to parts of the area, but that more than 30 sites remained closed as of 7:30 a.m. ACDT Friday (3:00 p.m. CT Thursday), including schools in Cleve, Lock, Penong, and Streaky Bay. InDaily, citing an Education Department statement, said around 35 schools were impacted with closures on Friday and that schools in the Port Lincoln area were expected to open, with school buses there operating as normal. Premier Peter Malinauskas said Friday that rapid engineering and design work was underway on a temporary solution for the downed transmission line, and that cranes had been secured to re-erect the line if it was deemed safe. Treasurer and Energy Minister Tom Koutsantonis said Port Lincoln was islanded and asked people in that region with solar panels to safely turn them off because they could trip the temporary generators supplying the township. SA Power Networks, in an 8:30 a.m. Friday, Nov 20 update (4:00 p.m. CT Thursday) on its Eyre Peninsula outages page, said it was seeking the support of customers on Eyre Peninsula who had power restored overnight, that those customers were being supplied by temporary generation while ElectraNet continued to repair its network, and that customers with rooftop solar that exports to the network should switch off their inverters and leave them off until SA Power Networks asked them to turn them back on. ABC also cited Telstra as saying there were currently total outages across 22 sites in the Eyre Peninsula. Earlier Thursday figures, from an ABC News article posted at 5:59 p.m. ACDT Thursday, Nov 19, and updated at 10:33 p.m. ACDT Thursday (6:03 a.m. CT), said about 24,000 properties on the peninsula lost power after two major transmission towers were downed and a third was damaged at Wharminda, and that by 10:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (5:30 a.m. CT) the number of properties without power had fallen to about 14,500. That Thursday 10:00 p.m. figure is earlier than the Friday 9:00 a.m. remaining count. ABC said that as of 7:00 p.m. ACDT Thursday (2:30 a.m. CT), almost 29,000 properties across the state were without electricity, about 24,000 of them on Eyre Peninsula, and that the same wild weather later started to hit Adelaide, causing more than 3,000 properties to lose power because of suspected storm-related faults on the distribution network. The South Australian Department for Energy and Mining said severe weather had caused significant damage to electricity transmission infrastructure, including the destruction of two transmission towers on the 132 kV line between Cultana and Port Lincoln, disrupting supply to communities across the central and lower Eyre Peninsula and West Coast. It said the Minister for Energy and Mining had declared an electricity supply emergency under the Emergency Management Act 2004, that the declaration took effect at 6:15 p.m. ACDT Thursday (1:45 a.m. CT), and that it will remain in force for 14 days unless revoked earlier. Malinauskas said Thursday that eight hospitals across Eyre Peninsula and Ceduna on the West Coast had been affected and were operating on generators; InDaily, in its Friday report, quoted him as saying all of the hospitals' backup power systems had worked appropriately. AEMO Market Notice 146498, issued at 8:42 p.m. NEM time Thursday (8:12 p.m. ACDT Thursday, 3:42 a.m. CT) and reported by WattClarity, directed the Port Lincoln generating unit to synchronise and maintain local load and frequency control in an islanded system from 8:35 p.m. NEM time Thursday until further notice.
