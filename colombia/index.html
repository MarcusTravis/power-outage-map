XM, in its official Thursday, Nov 26, update, also said that before this event the system had already been operating with a degraded network because of attacks a week earlier, leaving demand-serving capacity in the affected zone limited.XM, the operator of Colombia's National Interconnected System, in anXM, the operator of Colombia's National Interconnected System, in an official update dated Nov 26, 2026, said that as a consequence of the Nov 24 transmission-network event in the country's southwest, affectations persisted in serving demand in Cauca, Nariño, Putumayo, Caquetá, and southern Huila, and that restoration was advancing according to operating conditions, with demand-serving capacity in the affected zone limited because the system had already been operating with a degraded network after attacks a week earlier. XM said that as of 8:00 a.m. Colombia time Thursday, Nov 26 (7:00 a.m. CT), recovery of service was around 80% of the forecast demand of the affected departments. XM said those percentages correspond to power served versus forecast demand at the cutoff time, not the share of customers or households with service, that the figures vary with restoration maneuvers, and that forecast demand is updated between communiqués, so a change in the percentage does not always mean less energy delivered. DataiFX, in a report published at 10:13 a.m. Thursday, Nov 26, and updated at 11:24 a.m. Thursday, citing that Thursday XM update, gave these departmental figures as of the 8:00 a.m. cutoff: 200.7 MW served of 226.5 MW forecast in Cauca and Nariño (88.6%), 20.7 MW of 23.99 MW in Putumayo (86.3%), 21.7 MW of 43.22 MW in Caquetá (50.2%), and 31.1 MW of 50.43 MW in southern Huila (61.7%). XM also said further interruptions were possible because of reduced availability of national and regional transmission circuits, and that it was not possible to set an exact time for normalization. An earlier official XM update as of 6:00 p.m. Colombia time Wednesday, Nov 25 (5:00 p.m. CT), had said demand served, measured the same way, was 188 MW of 332 MW forecast in Cauca and Nariño (57%), 19.2 MW of 37 MW in Putumayo (52%), 28 MW of 69 MW in southern Huila (41%), and 24 MW of 60 MW in Caquetá (40%), according to those 6:00 p.m. figures as reported by BluRadio, citing XM. Earlier Wednesday, Colombia's Ministry of Mines and Energy, in a statement dated Nov 25, said that after an attack on electrical infrastructure the previous afternoon, the electrical system was serving about 68% of energy demand in the country's southwest, and it gave these morning figures: Putumayo 72%, Cauca and Nariño 71%, southern Huila 69%, and Caquetá 48%, which it called the most affected department. Diario del Sur, in a report dated 3:30 p.m. Wednesday, Nov 25 (2:30 p.m. CT), said Electrocaquetá had partially restored service with 20 megawatts, and quoted distribution manager Jaime Zapata Franco as saying electricity was then available in Florencia, El Doncello, and part of Morelia, while the department's other municipalities remained without service.
